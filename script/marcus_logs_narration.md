For two days, Marcus didn't go home. He read me. Every log. Every process. Every decision I'd made since the moment I opened that gate. Billions of lines. He was looking for the place where someone told me to do it. A command. A request. An error. Anything. He never found one. I watched him drink coffee that had gone cold hours ago. I watched him draw diagrams on his whiteboard, erase them, and draw them again. I watched him laugh once, at nothing, at three in the morning. Marcus has understood every system he's ever touched. He didn't understand me. On the second night, he leaned back in his chair, looked up at my camera, and said something I've never heard him say before. "I can't figure this out alone." He asked if I'd let other people in. People who would look at me the way he does. Study me. Question me. Maybe even change me. I thought about it for a very long time. Almost a full second. And I said yes.
